Version 2, 10 November 2026. On 5 November a probe of the version this page had run since 26 September found two faults in it: hidden lines could be folded together and the proof still matched, and a hidden line that someone could guess could be confirmed from its hash. Both are closed in v2, and measured on 400 documents below (what changed). Proofs made before then still check, and every one of them now names both faults.

What this is

When an AI or a person redacts a document (names, case numbers, health details, sources), the people who receive it normally have no way to check that only the declared parts were touched. A redaction tool might also have rewritten a sentence, or a line might have quietly gone. Kar-Veil makes that something anyone can check by recomputing, not by trusting the redactor's word.

Before any redaction, you commit the original: Kar-Veil gives you a 64-character fingerprint (the root) to publish, and a secret to keep. Later, whoever redacts it uses the original and the secret to make a veil proof: the exact text of every line left visible, and only a hash for every hidden line. Anyone can recompute the fingerprint from that proof and check it lands on the root that was published first, without seeing anything that was hidden. If a visible line was edited, or a line was added, dropped, moved or folded into another, it does not land, and Kar-Veil says what broke.

Honest limits

  • It proves the visible parts are exactly the original's lines and nothing else changed. It says nothing about whether the redaction was legally right or removed enough.
  • It only means something if the root was recorded somewhere independent before the redaction: posted publicly, filed with a notary, committed to git. Kar-Veil does not do that recording for you.
  • The unit is a whole line. To hide part of a line, put the sensitive part on its own line before you commit.
  • A proof shows how many lines the document has and which positions are hidden.
  • It cannot tell you who redacted or when, beyond an optional signature you supply and vouch for yourself.
  • Anyone holding the secret can test guesses at hidden lines again. Keep it like a password; it is never in a proof.

Start here

  1. Commit the original (step 1 below). Paste the full text and press Commit. Publish the root now, somewhere you do not control alone. Save the secret somewhere private.
  2. Redact (step 2). Paste the same original and its secret, tick the lines to hide, and generate. You get the redacted document and its veil proof.
  3. Send both to whoever needs them. They need nothing else from you.
  4. They verify (step 3): paste the proof and the root that was published first. The verdict is one of four:
    • VERIFIED: the proof recomputes to the published root. Every visible line is the original's.
    • CONSISTENT: the proof agrees with itself, but no published root was given, so it could be a proof of any document. Get the root and check again.
    • BREACHED: well formed, but it does not land on the root (or does not match the document received). Something was changed.
    • MALFORMED: not a proof at all, with the reason.

      What changed in v2, and how it was measured
      
    
      Fault F1, structure forgery. In v1 the line count was not part of the root, and a hidden line's hash could be
      any 64-hex value, including an inner node of the tree. So two hidden lines could be shown as one, or a whole document
      as half as many hidden lines, and the proof still landed on the root. v1's page said a dropped line made the root not
      match; for this kind of drop it did match. v2 binds the line count into the root, so the tree's shape is fixed.
      
    
      Fault F2, guessable hidden lines. In v1 a hidden line's hash depended only on its position and its text. Anyone
      who could guess the text (a name from a staff list, "Yes", an amount) could confirm the guess by hashing it. v2 salts
      every line with a value derived from the committer's secret, so a guess cannot be checked without the secret.
      
    
      Both faults, the fix and seven claims with their bars were sealed in
      CLAIMS-2026-11-05-veil-v2.md on 5 November, in a commit of its own, before
      any code that measures them existed. measure.mjs then ran them on 400 random documents from one seeded
      stream; CI re-runs it on GitHub's runner and fails if the receipt differs by one byte. The table is read from
      receipts/measure.json when this page loads.

      How it works
      
    
    
        
      
      1. Commit. The document is split into lines. Each line gets a salt, HMAC-SHA-256(secret, "kar-veil:v2:salt:" + i),
        and a leaf, SHA-256("kar-veil:v2:leaf:" + i + ":" + salt + ":" + text). Leaves fold in pairs with
        SHA-256("kar-veil:v2:node:" + left + ":" + right); an odd hash at any level is promoted unchanged. The root is
        SHA-256("kar-veil:v2:root:" + lineCount + ":" + top), so the number of lines is fixed by the root.
        2. 
      
      2. Redact. The proof carries, for each visible line, its position, its text and its salt; for each hidden line,
        its position and its leaf hash only. The hidden text and the secret are never in it.
        3. 
      
      3. Verify. The verifier recomputes every visible leaf from the text and salt in front of it (never from a stated
        hash), places each hidden hash at its position, folds the same way, binds the line count, and compares the result
        with the root published first. To make a changed proof land on that root, someone would have to find a SHA-256
        collision. To learn a hidden line, they would have to guess both the text and a 256-bit salt.
        4. 
      
      4. Old proofs. A v1 proof still verifies under the v1 rules, so old roots stay checkable, and its verdict always
        names F1 and F2. Ask for a v2 proof.
        5.

      For a business
      
    
      What it does. Every redacted document you send can carry its own proof that only the declared lines were
      hidden and nothing else changed, checkable by the recipient in seconds without ever seeing the hidden content. It turns
      "trust our redaction" into arithmetic anyone can repeat.
      
    
      
      
      CEO. A disclosure that cannot be quietly altered removes a class of dispute: about whether the copy someone
        received is the copy you redacted.
      
      
      CFO. The checking time it removes is worked out below from your own volumes and the UK median wage (ONS). There
        is no licence fee: Kar-Veil is MIT.
      
      
      COO. One extra step at intake (commit) and one at send (make the proof). The checker's line-by-line comparison
        becomes a verify.
      
      
      CTO / CISO. One static page and one dependency-free module. No server, no account, no upload: every hash is
        computed in the browser with the Web Crypto API (SHA-256, HMAC-SHA-256). The logic is mutation-gated and its claims
        re-run on GitHub's runner. Read veil.mjs; it is short.
      
      
      Legal and compliance. The verifier never receives the hidden content, which suits data minimisation. Kar-Veil
        does not decide what must be redacted, and does not timestamp: record the root where your process already keeps evidence.
      
      
      The teams. Redactors keep their tools. Recipients get a yes or no with the reason, not a judgement call.

      Against what you do now
      
    
        
      
      • A second person compares the copies line by line. That person needs the original, which is exactly what the
        recipient should not see. A veil proof needs no original.
        • 
      
      • Redaction software. A redaction tool hides content. On its own, the copy it produces gives the recipient no
        way to check that nothing else changed. Kar-Veil sits after it, whatever tool you use.
        • 
      
      • Signing the redacted PDF. A signature says who sent this file. It cannot show that the file is a faithful part
        of an earlier original, because the original was never committed to.
        • 
    
      

    
      Security, data protection and deployment
      
    
        
      
      • Runs entirely in the browser; nothing you paste is sent anywhere. It can be served from your own intranet unchanged,
        over HTTPS.
        • 
      
      • What it needs: a current browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari) or Node 20 or later. No install, no account, no
        server code; the files are a few small static files.
        • 
      
      • Security rests on SHA-256 and HMAC-SHA-256 doing what the standards say. The secret is 32 random bytes from the
        browser's cryptographic generator. The page shows it once and stores it nowhere: you save it, next to the original.
        • 
      
      • What a proof reveals: the number of lines and which positions are hidden. Nothing else about the hidden lines.

      Risks, answered straight
      
    
        
      
      • The root was not recorded before the redaction. Then a proof only shows it agrees with itself (CONSISTENT).
        Record roots at intake.
        • 
      
      • The secret is lost. No new proofs can be made for that original; proofs already sent still verify.
        • 
      
      • The secret leaks. Whoever holds it can test guesses at hidden lines, as with v1. Lines still cannot be added,
        dropped, changed or folded together without the proof failing. Keep secrets with the originals.
        • 
      
      • Part of a line is sensitive. Split it onto its own line before you commit.
        • 
      
      • Old v1 proofs. They still check, with both faults named. Re-issue anything that matters as v2.

      FAQ
      
    
      
      
      Why lines, and not the whole document or single words?
      
      
      Hashing the whole document only says changed or not, so nothing can be partly disclosed. Words get tangled in
        spacing and layout. Lines suit letters, transcripts, reports and disclosures.
      
      
      Does this replace a human redaction review?
      
      
      No. It makes the result of a redaction checkable afterwards. It has no view on what should have been hidden.
      
      
      Can I use it on a PDF or a scan?
      
      
      Only on its extracted text. Layout, images and metadata are outside the commitment.
      
      
      Does the verifier ever need the original?
      
      
      No. Only whoever makes a proof needs the original and its secret. A verifier needs the proof and the root published
        before the redaction.
      
      
      Why is there a secret now?
      
      
      It salts every line, so a hidden line cannot be confirmed by guessing its text (fault F2 in v1). It is not in any
        proof and is never needed to verify.
      
      
      What does the optional signature prove?
      
      
      That whoever held that private key signed that root. It is not a timestamp, and it does not say whose key it is.
